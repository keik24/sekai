🇭🇷 Croatia Public Holidays 2026–2027

Dates, weekdays and local names of public holidays in Croatia. Holidays observed only in some regions are marked.

2026

14 public holidays
  • Thu, Jan 1
    New Year's DayNova godina

    New Year's Day marks the start of the year in the Gregorian calendar and falls on 1 January. It is a public holiday in most countries and is widely observed with celebrations, family gatherings, and time off work.

  • Tue, Jan 6
    EpiphanyBogojavljenje ili Sveta tri kralja

    Epiphany is a Christian feast celebrating the manifestation of Jesus to the Gentiles, traditionally represented by the visit of the Magi. Western churches observe it on 6 January; Eastern churches focus on Jesus' baptism.

  • Sun, Apr 5
    Easter SundayUskrs

    Easter Sunday, the principal Christian feast, celebrates the resurrection of Jesus. It is the first Sunday after the church full moon on or after 21 March; Orthodox churches reckon it by the Julian calendar, so their date often differs.

  • Mon, Apr 6
    Easter MondayUskrsni ponedjeljak

    Easter Monday is the Christian holiday on the day after Easter Sunday, following Western or Orthodox Easter by country. It is a public holiday in much of Europe and in many Commonwealth and Latin American countries.

  • Fri, May 1
    Labour DayPraznik rada

    Labor Day is a public holiday honouring workers and the labour movement. In most countries it falls on 1 May (International Workers' Day); in the United States and Canada it is observed on the first Monday of September.

  • Sat, May 30
    Statehood DayDan državnosti

    Statehood Day marks a country's founding or restoration and falls on different dates, such as 15 February in Serbia, 30 May in Croatia, 25 June in Slovenia, 6 July in Lithuania, 13 July in Montenegro and 28 September in Czechia.

  • Thu, Jun 4
    Corpus ChristiTijelovo

    Corpus Christi is a Roman Catholic feast celebrating the real presence of Christ in the Eucharist; it is a public holiday in several countries.

  • Mon, Jun 22
    Anti-Fascist Struggle DayDan antifašističke borbe

    Anti-Fascist Struggle Day is a public holiday in Croatia on 22 June. It marks the formation of the first partisan detachment near Sisak in 1941, the start of armed resistance to the Axis occupation.

  • Wed, Aug 5
    Victory and Homeland Thanksgiving DayDan pobjede i domovinske zahvalnosti i Dan hrvatskih branitelja

    Victory and Homeland Thanksgiving Day, together with the Day of Croatian Defenders, is a public holiday in Croatia on 5 August. It marks the 1995 capture of Knin in Operation Storm, which effectively ended the War of Independence.

  • Sat, Aug 15
    Assumption of MaryVelika Gospa

    Assumption Day marks the Christian belief that Mary, mother of Jesus, was taken up into heaven, called the Dormition in Orthodox churches. It falls on 15 August, or on 28 August in churches using the Julian calendar.

  • Sun, Nov 1
    All Saints' DaySvi sveti

    All Saints' Day, the Christian feast of all saints, falls on 1 November in the West, but in Sweden and Finland on the Saturday from 31 October to 6 November. Most Eastern churches keep it on the first Sunday after Pentecost.

  • Wed, Nov 18
    Remembrance DayDan sjećanja na žrtve Domovinskog rata i Dan sjećanja na žrtvu Vukovara i Škabrnje

    Remembrance Day honours those who died in war. Bermuda, Canada and the Cayman Islands mark it around 11 November, the 1918 Armistice, while Croatia observes it on 18 November for the victims of the Homeland War and Vukovar.

  • Fri, Dec 25
    Christmas DayBožić

    Christmas Day is the Christian festival celebrating the birth of Jesus. Most countries observe it on 25 December, while churches following the Julian calendar celebrate it on 7 January. It is a public holiday in most countries.

  • Sat, Dec 26
    Saint Stephen's DaySveti Stjepan

    Saint Stephen's Day is a Christian feast commemorating Saint Stephen, the first Christian martyr. It is observed on 26 December in Western Christianity and is a public holiday in several European countries.
